    How Tape Runner works

    1. It watches the order book live

    The bot stays connected to Coinbase's public feed and keeps the full list of buy and sell orders waiting at every price (Level 2) for Bitcoin, Ethereum and Solana, updated many times a second.

    2. It scores buy vs sell pressure 4 times a second

    Three readings blend into one score from −1 to +1: depth (more size waiting on the buy side than the sell side?), order flow (are buyers adding and lifting at the best prices faster than sellers?), and trade flow (are market orders mostly buying?).

    3. It jumps in when the score is strong

    Past +0.55 for two checks in a row, it buys. Past −0.55, it takes a pretend short. Only when the spread is tight and the coin is actively trading.

    4. It's out within a minute

    Sells at +0.08% profit, −0.06% loss, if the order book flips hard against it (after 5 seconds), or after 60 seconds. Every trade crosses the spread both ways, like a real market order.

    • Pretend money, watch-only. Trades last seconds, far too fast for a person to copy from a phone. Shorts are pretend too; most regular crypto apps can't short.
    • The research: order-flow imbalance predicts very short-term price moves (Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov, 2014), and crypto order books show the same effect. But the moves are tiny, usually a few hundredths of a percent.
    • The catch is fees. Coinbase Advanced's entry fee in the US is 0.90% for orders that take the best price, so a round trip costs 1.80%. That's why there are two scoreboards. Professional scalpers pay a tiny fraction of that, or get paid to provide liquidity.
    • Shift changes: it runs on GitHub, which stops each job after 6 hours, so every ~5¾ hours it closes any open trade and a fresh run takes over about a minute later.
    • Not financial advice.
